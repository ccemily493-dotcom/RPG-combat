## 2. Architecture added

`resolveTurn` consumes one immutable world/character snapshot, normalized action and reaction declarations, validated registries/configuration, and a seed. It performs declaration validation, dependency-aware deterministic ordering, resource/action-economy reservation, target allocation, reaction processing, individual or batch hit resolution, secondary consequence collection, deterministic simultaneous merge, and one atomic commit.

The result contains the new immutable snapshot, action/reaction results, canonical events, conflicts, replay hashes, and an optional turn-level trace. Resolution order and commit are separated; simultaneous actions read the starting snapshot. Stable IDs are final tie-breakers and audit keys only.

The v0.1 `resolveEncounter` API remains available. The new API adds explicit character maps, action/reaction arrays, cost modes, target allocations, reaction scopes, registry schemas, and complete-turn replay.

## 3. Canonical v0.1.1 tuning changes applied

- Accuracy/evasion opposed weight totals are normalized independently.
- Weighted defense composition is canonical, with feasibility/cost/applicability gates, separate execution/timing/compatibility/coverage contributions, bounded stability, and full component tracing.
- Configurable damage gap ranges are non-blocking balance expectations.
- Reciprocal low harm-capacity amplification is replaced with a monotonic bounded curve: 1.00 at normal capacity, 1.10 at 0.75 ratio, 1.20 at 0.50, and a maximum 1.35 at or below 0.25.
- Centered triangular is canonical micro-variance on the inclusive 0.99–1.01 interval. Uniform and bounded normal-like variants remain selectable.
- Hard invariants and balance expectations are explicit separate validation classes.

No additional damage or defense coefficients were silently retuned.

## 8. Multi-hit results

Quantities 1, 3, 5, 10, 50, 100, and 500 are covered by direct tests or property cohorts. Small quantities resolve hit-by-hit. Safe large quantities use configured deterministic groups while preserving represented count, allocation, resource obligations, channels, and declared status approximation semantics. Micro-variance remains per deterministic component and is not used to create tactical diversity.

Cost modes `PER_ACTION`, `PER_HIT`, `SHARED_POOL`, and `UPFRONT_PLUS_PER_HIT` all have quantity-aware reservation and conservation tests.

## 9. Multi-target results

`SINGLE`, `EACH_TARGET`, and `DISTRIBUTED` target modes are normalized. Scenario D proves exact declared distribution across heterogeneous character snapshots. Undeclared allocation targets, allocation totals that do not equal quantity, and invalid target references fail before commit.

## 10. Reaction/interruption results

Reaction capacity is explicit state and may be zero, one, or many. Reservations prevent double consumption and negative capacity. Coverage can apply to one hit, selected hits, a bounded subset, an entire action, an area, or a window; a one-hit block cannot implicitly cover a 500-hit action.

Defense reactions retain the complete weighted-defense trace. Explicit interruption contracts support cancel, delay, reduced execution, altered target, broken concentration, and consumed reaction effects. Chain depth is bounded by configuration. Scenario H verifies a successful explicit cancel and its cost semantics.

## 11. Simultaneous-action results

Scenario E proves reciprocal attacks are both resolved from the starting snapshot rather than erased by incidental first damage. Reversing declaration-array order produces the same result hash and character state. Additive health/stability deltas commute. Status, action-economy, displacement, and incapacitation consequences merge in a deterministic commit stage.

## 16. Specification ambiguities discovered

- Cost behavior after interruption required an explicit distinction between `on_attempt` and `on_contact` obligations.
- Multi-target quantity needed explicit allocation modes; target arrays alone cannot say whether quantity is total or per target.
- Reaction coverage needed hit/action/area/window scope rather than a Boolean eligibility flag.
- Status merge policy had to move from action hints to formal registry definitions.
- Concurrent absolute displacement needed a conflict policy distinct from additive displacement.
- Batch safety required semantic exclusion reasons and equivalence tolerances, not quantity alone.
- Stable ordering required a declared dependency DAG and explicit timing/stat/priority stages before the ID tie-breaker.

Conservative configurable defaults for these points are recorded in `combat-loop.yaml`, the registry schemas, and `open-decisions.md`.

## 17. Provisional values that appear suspicious

- Extreme-gap damage mean 85.56 is below its 90–150 diagnostic band.
- Canonical defense p05 is 15.78%, close to the poor/weak diagnostic boundary; the capacity stage remains the largest collapse at 46.64 percentage points.
- Several specialized and unequal-category accuracy cohorts remain entirely in one band. This follows modeled margins and deliberately tiny variance, but intended outcome rates still need human targets.
- Batch approximation is extremely close in the tested homogeneous volley, but is not byte-identical to forced individual damage/stability and must remain tolerance-guarded.
- Fighter versus Civilian remains a high 50.45-damage outcome even after bounded vulnerability and weighted defense; whether that is desirable is a tuning decision, not a correctness failure.

## 18. Remaining core limitations

- The engine resolves complete individual turns but does not yet provide a cross-turn scheduler, cooldown progression service, or long-form combat session controller.
- Interruption ordering is discrete and declaration-driven, not a continuous event clock.
- Geometry conflict handling is deterministic but deliberately minimal; there is no collision, occupancy, pathfinding, or physics engine.
- Batching supports homogeneous cases that pass the safety contract; stateful per-hit statuses/reactions/interruption remain individual.
- Registry schemas validate structure, but there is no registry authoring or compatibility tool.
- No persistent event store, networking, multiplayer server, strategy/Ability Parser, LLM, universe extension, named ability, advanced anatomy, or death/dismemberment system is present.
